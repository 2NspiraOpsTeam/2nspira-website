## 2. Compliance operating standard
- Include the required statement: “As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.” Add a plain-language commission disclosure near recommendations and links, including each relevant social post.
- No self-purchases, incentivized clicks, fabricated reviews, invented personal testing, copied Amazon reviews, or misleading scarcity. Do not ask friends or staff to buy to qualify the account.
- No manual stale prices, ratings, stock claims, or scraped Amazon product images. Use original/licensed imagery or an expressly permitted Amazon integration. Current launch uses text and manufacturer source citations.
- Keep Special Links on permitted registered properties. Email/SMS/social-message use is conditional on Amazon’s current policy, opt-in and easy opt-out; it is not a blanket prohibition. Initial email strategy points to useful website guides, with consent-based subscriptions only.
- Mark paid/affiliate outbound links `rel="sponsored nofollow"`. Do not cloak the destination or insert personal identifiers into tracking tags.
- No paid search bidding on Amazon marks or paid direct-to-Amazon campaigns. No paid acquisition in this plan.
- Current Creators API eligibility and credentials are separate from SiteStripe access. Do not build an API-dependent launch until approved access is verified. Current inspected API guidance included a sales eligibility requirement; no keys were created.
- Publish useful original analysis with compatibility criteria and alternatives. No mass-produced near-duplicate “best product” pages.

## 3. Top five niche hypotheses
These are prioritization hypotheses based on audience fit and available evidence, not measured keyword-volume or profitability rankings.
1. Home-office connectivity and productive desk setups: strong fit with 2Nspira technology work; useful compatibility decisions; avoid competing solely on broad laptop keywords.
2. Paperless small-business workflows: clear operational problem; scanners and supporting storage/organization; research must cover workflow rather than only hardware.
3. Practical creator and remote-meeting equipment: webcams, microphones, lighting; publish only use-case-specific comparisons and label untested products.
4. Landlord/property-operation tools: inspection and organization kits; later expansion after audience evidence, avoiding professional safety claims.
5. Learning and family project kits/books: aligns with existing books audience; lower order values require more volume; keep separate editorial collections.

Start with niches 1–2 on ONE hub, not five separate websites. Expand only after traffic and conversion evidence.

## 6. Channel strategy
Primary: an owned /buying-guides hub with evergreen search-oriented articles, supporting workflow content, internal discovery and a sitemap. Maintain existing service positioning; the hub is a supporting resource, not a replacement homepage.
Secondary: YouTube demonstrations and Instagram/Facebook short educational posts adapted from genuinely useful articles. Register eligible exact profiles before affiliate-link distribution. Do not claim recorded demos until they exist.
Retention: optional, permission-based newsletter linking back to guides. No purchased lists or unsolicited outreach.
Do not automate external posting before account ownership, permissions, destinations and disclosure formatting are verified. Do not build a multi-domain content network before one hub works.

## 7. Thirty-day execution plan
- Days 1–7: launch hub, two researched guides, disclosure and editorial standard; verify SiteStripe links, mobile layout, sitemap and internal navigation. Inspect existing analytics configuration without exposing credentials. Establish a baseline report (unknown is not zero).
- Days 8–14: publish 2–3 additional guides only after the evidence/link checklist passes. Prepare 4–6 derivative social drafts; verify/register owned eligible profiles before distribution. Establish guide-level outbound measurement using the existing consent/analytics approach.
- Days 15–21: publish 2–3 more validated guides; improve early pages based on actual queries and audience questions. Produce original demonstrations only for equipment actually available. No purchase budget assumed.
- Days 22–30: review impressions, qualified visits, clicks, Amazon shipped items, credited commissions, returns and earnings per click. Update weak sections, fix broken links, and choose next-month topics based on observed demand. Aim for 6–8 substantial guides, not a quota of thin pages.

## 8. Repeatable operating loop
Weekly: inspect demand → brief → research → verify exact product → generate link → editorial review → publish → verify rendered output → distribute on authorized channels → measure → improve.
Maintain an inventory with URL, problem, source date, ASIN, tracking ID, link proof, publication state, and next review date. Recheck published links monthly and whenever a product changes. Remove broken offers promptly; remove/update expired promotions.

Scorecard: week/date; published URLs; indexed URLs (when Search Console access is proven); organic impressions/clicks; guide visits; unique outbound clicks; Associates ordered/shipped items; credited commissions; reversals; EPC. Avoid mixing analytics clicks with Amazon clicks as if they were the same measurement. No individual shopper identifiers.

Scale gate: evidence of demand and credited conversions, current disclosures, verified link health, and a maintainable review workload. Stop expanding an unproductive niche and improve intent matching first.
