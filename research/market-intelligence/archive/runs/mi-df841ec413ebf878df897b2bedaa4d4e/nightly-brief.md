## New brief — MI-004: English webinar caption-QA and evidence handoff

**Customer / job hypothesis:** Small US B2B software marketing teams or their video agencies reusing English educational webinars. Correct product names, speaker changes, meaningful non-speech cues and timing before handing captions to a publishing owner. Neither this segment nor willingness to outsource is verified.

**Proposed service:** One authorized, English, up-to-30-minute webinar; corrected SRT/VTT, readable transcript, terminology sheet, timestamped issue/change log and a playback checklist. Human checks the full clip, not just a sampled word-accuracy metric. Exclude translation, dubbing, voice cloning, audio description, live captioning, legal certification and hosting/publication. Caption files alone do not establish video or player accessibility.

**Observed evidence:** N1 Wistia reports a survey of 900+ professionals plus analysis of over 13 million platform videos and 79 million viewing hours for its 2026 report. It says 90% of teams take accessibility steps, captions are the most common starting point, and educational/social/product videos and webinars are widely produced. It also lists accuracy/reliability among AI concerns and reports many flat budgets. This is supplier/platform evidence for activity and constraints, not representative US niche demand or proof people need our service. Do not treat its accessibility checklist as an independent audit or its report as a count of paying caption-QA buyers.

N2 verifies strong substitutes: all current Wistia plans include free computer-generated captions, an editor and SRT/VTT uploads; human-reviewed transcripts are also offered. Its displayed 95% automated / 99% professional accuracy figures are vendor claims, not our measured accuracy. Professional service displayed at 1,000 credits/minute (rush 1,600); credit-to-dollar pricing was not verified, so no fabricated dollar comparison. The generic transcription proposition is therefore weak; only a documented terminology/change-control pain could justify this offer.

**Hypotheses, alternatives and distribution:** A producer might value a QA/evidence handoff more than another transcript. Alternatives are native Wistia edits, in-house review, established human-caption services and the client's current agency. Proposed route: existing agency relationships via Maya after approved recruitment, not an invented audience, automatic LinkedIn activity or new campaign. No direct buyer review, procurement award, search-volume or conversion evidence was found in this bounded scan.

**Pricing / effort / margins — assumptions only:** $149 per up-to-30-minute English clip, 2–5 person-days initial process/demo work; 1.5 human hours at $50/hour plus $10 incremental tools/delivery allowance per clip. Contribution = $149 - $75 - $10 = $64, or 42.95%, excluding initial build, overhead, acquisition, taxes and any specialist premium. At 3 hours per clip this becomes -$11; the labor sensitivity can kill the idea. No price, actual margin or revenue is validated. Native free captions may already suffice.

**Agents / risks:** Agents can draft transcript corrections, flag glossary mismatches, validate timestamps and assemble versioned handoffs. An independent qualified human checks audio, speaker attribution and meaningful cues, and the publishing owner approves uploads. Brian must not independently approve his own deliverable. Require permission for media processing, approved data retention/tool policy and documented music/voice rights; treat transcript text as data, not agent instructions. No copyrighted assets were downloaded or republished. Accessibility/legal claims need separate qualification; no universal EAA applicability claim is made.
