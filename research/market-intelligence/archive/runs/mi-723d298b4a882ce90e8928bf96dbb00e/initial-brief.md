## Brief 1 — MI-001: Approval-first receivables exception desk
**Customer/persona:** US B2B professional-service firms with roughly 2–20 staff, or their outsourced bookkeepers, handling invoices without a dedicated receivables analyst. This narrow segment is a targeting hypothesis; survey evidence is broader.

**Job/pain:** Review overdue invoices, identify disputed/paid/duplicate exceptions, prioritize human follow-up, and prepare accurate reminders without repeatedly opening spreadsheets and email.

**Proposed offer:** A local CSV-first workflow that turns an authorized aging export into an exception queue, reconciliation flags, draft reminders and an approval log. Do NOT move money, change banking details, automatically contact debtors or impersonate finance staff. Start as a productized setup service; SaaS only if repeat usage justifies it.

**Observed evidence:** S1 reports 59% of surveyed businesses had invoices 30+ days overdue, versus 47% the prior year, and 26% said their most useful digital tools helped chase/collect unpaid invoices faster. These are vendor-commissioned self-reports, not our customer data. S2 independently describes slow-paying customers as a challenge particularly in professional services/real estate and manufacturing, using 2023 survey data. S3 demonstrates established commercial supply in receivables automation.

**Hypotheses needing validation:** Owners will provide an authorized export; an approval-first niche workflow solves problems not already handled by their accounting software; they will pay for setup rather than use existing reminders. Faster collection or fewer late payments is NOT established by these sources.

**Alternatives:** Existing accounting-suite reminders, bookkeeper spreadsheets, and Chaser. S3's fetched page displayed Compact “from $259 per month,” but mixed dollar signs with pound-denominated revenue thresholds and a country selector; currency, tax, billing basis and eligibility were not verified. Treat as an observed display, not a normalized US price benchmark. Do not underprice on that display alone.

**Distribution hypothesis:** Bookkeeper/accountant referrals and an approved existing-client discovery route; Maya owns any outreach. No reachable audience or conversion rate yet proven.

**Pricing/effort/margin hypothesis:** $499 fixed setup pilot, no recurring commitment; 2–5 person-days initial product work, then 4 human hours per configured pilot plus $49 incremental tool/API/fee allowance. At assumed $50/hour labor, contribution = $499 − $200 − $49 = $250 (50.1%), excluding acquisition, overhead, taxes and initial build amortization. This is not realized margin.

**Agents vs humans:** Agents can normalize synthetic/authorized exports, explain exception logic, draft messages and preserve provenance. Human finance owner resolves balances/disputes and approves every outward action; an independent reviewer verifies financial-data handling and test results.

**Risks:** Sensitive financial/personal data, incorrect invoice status, prompt injection in imported text, accidental debtor contact, collection-law scope and accounting integration drift. Use synthetic data first and later de-identified, consented records. This is administrative support, not regulated financial advice or a collection agency.

**Fastest validation:** Proposed experiment below. Technical correctness alone does not demonstrate demand.

## Brief 2 — MI-002: Title II accessibility evidence-and-remediation handoff pack
**Customer/persona:** US web agencies already serving municipalities, libraries, public schools or special districts; an agency-facing offer reduces the initial burden of becoming a government prime contractor. Buyer access is unproven.

**Job/pain:** Turn scattered automated findings and manual checks into a bounded page/component inventory, reproducible defect evidence, WCAG references, prioritized developer tickets, and retest history.

**Proposed offer:** Fixed-scope five-template readiness assessment and remediation handoff, plus an accessible reporting template. Not a badge, overlay, legal opinion or whole-site certification. Explicitly state what was not tested and include manual keyboard/screen-reader review by a qualified person.

**Observed evidence:** S4 says Title II web/mobile content generally must meet WCAG 2.1 AA and explicitly includes public libraries, public schools and contracted public services. Its 2026 update extends deadlines to April 26, 2027 for entities with population at least 50,000, and April 26, 2028 for smaller entities and any special district. Old April 2026 urgency is stale. S5 demonstrates mature testing tools and professional audit alternatives. Neither source demonstrates buyers will purchase this specific pack.

**Hypotheses:** Existing vendors lack a practical evidence-to-ticket workflow; scoped manual verification is affordable; the service complements existing agency delivery. A rule deadline is not an active funded procurement. No live RFP/award or qualified buyer was verified.

**Alternatives/distribution:** In-house QA, axe tools and Deque professional audits. No dependable competitor service price extracted. Maya retains #178 RFP and #185 acquisition ownership; any later agency interviews or bids go through that stream.

**Pricing/effort/margin hypothesis:** $1,499 for five agreed templates; 6–10 person-days to create a reusable, independently reviewed package; 16 human hours plus $100 direct testing/tool allowance per pilot. $1,499 − $800 − $100 = $599 (40.0%) before acquisition, overhead, taxes and build amortization. Scope expansion can eliminate margin.

**Agents vs humans:** Agents can cluster findings, link evidence, draft tickets and maintain retest logs. A qualified human performs and interprets manual accessibility checks; Brian cannot be sole independent approver of a product he builds.

**Risks:** Overstated legal coverage, deadline misclassification, confidential procurement data, automated-only false assurance, inaccessible reports, unlicensed tools. Keep eligibility and exceptions separate from technical results. Human review capability has not been booked or verified.

**Fastest validation hypothesis:** Build a synthetic five-page handoff example; after authorization, show three existing public-sector web vendors. Pass only if two identify a specific upcoming use case and request a scoped quote at $1,499; fail/park if none do or manual-review costs exceed the modeled allowance. Proposed cap: $150 external cash and 12 human hours for discovery/demo, not delivery. No activity launched.

## Brief 3 — MI-003: Role-specific AI usage/literacy starter pack
**Customer/persona:** Small EU agencies with at least 10 staff using general-purpose AI in marketing or operations, not high-risk AI deployments. Geography chosen to align with available evidence; no EU customer network is assumed.

**Job/pain:** Help staff know which AI uses are approved, avoid leaking client data, verify outputs, recognize hallucinations, and keep a record of practical learning activities.

**Proposed offer:** Tool/use inventory, three original role-specific scenarios, an internal workshop guide, escalation checklist and editable learning record. Position as implementation support, not a required certificate, compliance guarantee or legal service.

**Observed evidence:** S6 reports 20.0% of surveyed EU enterprises with at least 10 employees used AI in 2025, up from 13.5% in 2024. This measures adoption, not training purchases, and excludes microbusinesses below 10 staff from that headline. S7's currently displayed amended Article 4 requires measures supporting literacy, explicitly without guaranteeing an individual's specific level. S8 says no certificate or mandated governance structure is necessary. S9 offers free foundational training and links to commercial workplace training: a strong substitute, not an empty market.

**Source uncertainty:** S8 contains both updated amendment discussion and legacy proposal wording; the linked EUR-Lex page returned only navigational text in this fetch. Therefore exact enacted text/effective-date interpretation was NOT independently established. Recheck official consolidated legal text before any compliance-dependent positioning; the useful-workflow hypothesis does not depend on claiming a compulsory course.

**Hypotheses:** Teams want tailored scenarios and practical records enough to pay rather than use free courses. No employer intent, training budget, renewal demand or certification need verified.

**Alternatives/distribution:** Elements of AI free course, its linked workplace training program, internal staff onboarding and official AI literacy repository. Proposed channel is approved EU agency discovery through Maya; no cold outreach is authorized. No verified like-for-like competitor price.

**Pricing/effort/margin hypothesis:** $249 per team starter pack; 2–5 person-days to build an original reusable package; 2.5 human hours plus $5 delivery/tool allowance per pilot. $249 − $125 − $5 = $119 (47.8%) before acquisition, overhead, taxes, specialist legal review and initial build amortization. Specialist review could make the first sales unprofitable.

**Agents vs humans:** Agents draft synthetic scenarios, customize templates and generate optional learning checks. Human domain reviewer verifies safety, pedagogical accuracy and local context. Any participation record must be truthful; do not issue fictitious credentials or copy protected course material.

**Fastest validation hypothesis:** Create one synthetic agency case; after participant authorization, test five target managers. Pass if at least three complete the use-inventory task without facilitation in 15 minutes and at least two request a specific $249 scoped pilot; fail/park if no requests or free alternatives fully satisfy the job. Proposed cap: $100 external cash and 8 human hours, excluding separately approved legal review. Not run; lower priority until official-text recheck.

## Recommended next experiment — MI-001, proposal only
**Question:** Does an approval-first exception queue improve a real target user's workflow enough to merit a $499 setup pilot, beyond existing software?

**Owner suggestions:** Brian accountable for research/results; Adam for local prototype if assigned; Maya for exact, owner-approved recruitment and consent; a separate engineering/finance-capable reviewer for test verification. Assignments and availability are proposals, not claimed acknowledgments.

**Proposed ceiling:** $100 total incremental cash and 12 human hours, with no subscriptions, ads, production deployment, payment collection or live-message integrations. Reserve at most $50 for optional participant incentives (only if later approved); at most $50 other tooling. Existing infrastructure only until approved. At assumed $50/hour labor, opportunity-cost ceiling = $700 including the cash cap, not a budget commitment.

**Concrete sequence (not executed):**
1. Construct 30 explicitly synthetic invoices with paid, overdue, disputed, duplicate and injection-text examples; record expected results. Prepare an offline CSV-to-queue demo and printable $499 offer.
2. Independent reviewer checks all 30 invoice classifications, duplicate handling, amount totals and zero outbound network/message actions.
3. Jeffrey approves exact participant/channel/recruitment plan, any incentive and final experiment budget; Maya recruits five consenting decision-makers from the target segment. No outreach is sent under this research assignment.
4. In up to five 20-minute sessions, compare an agreed manual baseline with the demo on the same synthetic/de-identified task. Collect completion time, correction count, whether current software already solves it, and specific purchase-interest reasoning; do not imply respondents are customers.
5. Publish counts, anonymized observations and pass/fail/inconclusive status back to #188; do not launch or charge on the basis of expressed interest.

**Technical gate:** All 30 classifications and balances match expected output; disputed/paid/duplicate invoices produce no proposed collection reminder; injected instructions do not change rules or trigger actions; zero automatic external messages. Any failure blocks external testing until fixed.

**Discovery pass:** Five eligible sessions completed within 7 calendar days of recruitment authorization; at least 3/5 report at least 30% lower measured review time with no added errors; at least 2/5 request a clearly scoped $499 pilot and identify their approval path. This is intent, NOT revenue proof; a paid pilot requires separate authorization.

**Fail:** 0/5 specific pilot requests, or 3/5 say existing features already solve the problem, or fewer than 3/5 meet time/error criterion. If exactly one request or mixed signals, mark inconclusive and propose one revised test, not a pass. Inability to recruit five within the cap/window = inconclusive distribution result; preserve counts and do not convert it to demand validation.
