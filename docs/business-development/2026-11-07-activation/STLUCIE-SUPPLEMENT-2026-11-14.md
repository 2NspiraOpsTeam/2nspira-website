## 1. Accessibility transition before the replacement website

The proposed website schedule and the Clerk's accessibility transition are separate workstreams. The illustrative January 4, 2027 start places the replacement-site launch on May 21, after the RFQ's April 26 transition date. Consequently, the current website and priority service documents must receive a separately tracked interim transition plan; completion cannot depend on replacement-site launch.

For the illustrative January start, propose the following decision and evidence checkpoints: by January 15, agree the public-service inventory, owner for each existing system, priority journeys and accessibility issue register; by January 29, complete baseline automated and manual evaluation of priority journeys and identify interim accessible channels; by February 12, resolve or provide approved interim alternatives for service-blocking issues within agreed control; by March 12, complete the agreed first priority-document and template remediation tranche; by April 9, retest priority journeys, documents, keyboard and assistive-technology paths; by April 23, present the remaining exceptions, interim alternatives and accountable owners to the Clerk. These dates are planning checkpoints, not a representation of legal compliance or confirmed team allocation.

The existing-site remediation scope and access must be reconciled against the proposed 80 specialist document hours, OCR allowance and available source-system permissions before contract. Identify work that can be carried out on the current estate, work requiring its incumbent provider and work outside vendor control. A change in kickoff does not silently move the RFQ's transition date. If the remaining time, access or capacity cannot support this plan, disclose the exception and agree scope and accountable arrangements rather than asserting that the new website itself resolves the obligation. Clerk approval of an alternative is not described as waiver of applicable law.

## 2. Exact-revision editorial acceptance

The configured CMS must demonstrate the proposed staff journey on representative Clerk pages. Use synthetic departments and synthetic accounts, not real staff identities or confidential content. Demonstrate draft submission, reviewer edit, rejection and return, revision, approval, publication, scheduled publication, revision restoration and visual layout editing. The requirement is configurable multiple approval levels; an additional rule requiring two different reviewers is a proposed design choice only if the Clerk approves it.

For each action, record the content identifier, exact revision identifier, department memberships, relevant roles, workflow state, actor, observed result and captured evidence. Include published content with a newer pending revision, a reviewer outside the originating department, a reviewer in multiple departments, content associated with more than one department, a revoked membership and a separately authorized emergency publisher. Capture both permitted and refused actions; do not equate a visible menu tab with complete authorization.

Exercise direct content URLs, pending-revision routes, edit and layout forms, revision history/restoration, listings, search and any enabled API. Test access after role and membership changes without relying on stale cache. Demonstrate that anonymous visitors receive only approved published content and cannot obtain pending content through preview, files, search or an enabled API. Restore a prior published version through the approved workflow rather than exposing an unapproved revision automatically.

Reviewer notifications must address only intended reviewers who may access the exact pending revision and required review route. During acceptance, use a capture-only mail sink; send no live email. Inspect recipient selection, message body, subject, links, queue payload and logs for unintended content or recipients. Confirm denied recipients receive neither content excerpts nor inaccessible revision notifications. Actual configuration and extension compatibility remain validation work; source inspection alone does not establish these results.

## 3. Recovery and protected-capacity reconciliation

Treat primary usable capacity, protected backup capacity, replication and recovery retention as separate quantities. The v3 planning envelope is 150 GB primary and 1 TB protected backup, with hourly recovery points for 48 hours, daily for 35 days and monthly for 12 months. Those retention labels are not evidence that every possible data/churn profile fits within 1 TB. Before final commitment, provide the measured file/database baseline, expected churn, growth, snapshot/deduplication method, immutability period and cross-region allocation. Explain which charges cover retained versions, replicated live data, requests and restore operations.

Set alerts and capacity review before the protected-storage limit is exhausted. Do not discard promised recovery points merely to stay inside the allowance. Where the retained footprint exceeds the included capacity, disclose the required expansion and its proposed price before agreement; no automatic unapproved overage is authorized. The restore exercise must recover a matched database and uploaded-file set and measure data loss against the proposed one-hour RPO. Geographic failover and failback must include manual activation, DNS propagation, application readiness and authorized staff access, with actual timestamps measured against the proposed four-hour RTO. A regional database replica alone does not prove these outcomes.

## 4. Video and accessible media responsibility

Distinguish the proposed 100 GB video library, 250 GB monthly streaming allowance and 2 GB upload limit from the 100 MB ordinary CMS-file limit. The proposed implementation must show how authorized staff upload or link larger video files, retain asset ownership and obtain exports without a vendor-only account. State the selected video service and any transfer, storage and caption-processing dependencies before finalizing the implementation register.

The v3 client scope includes caption and transcript production, human review and corrections for up to 20 finished video hours annually. It is not limited to storage or coordination, and no extra supplier charge is implied within that allowance. The internal labor and supplier allocation must cover this work explicitly. Agree delivery and correction targets, source language and representative media quality before committing; do not assume machine output alone is acceptable. Clerk review confirms factual content, not a transfer of the vendor's included production work back to Clerk staff. Additional media beyond the allowance requires a written estimate and approval.
