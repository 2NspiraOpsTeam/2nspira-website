# PEFC — service governance, continuity and handover response

PRIVATE EDITABLE INTERNAL DRAFT · 14 November 2026, 06ET · PARTIAL / BLOCKED

This proposed operating approach supplements the 04ET migration/design narrative and 05ET delivery/acceptance appendix; it is not a standalone partial-scope offer. It addresses the service obligations of PEFC-WEB2026-001 without claiming staffed coverage or deployed capability. Source citations below use physical PDF pages. Costs, personnel, service levels and contract terms remain unapproved. Do not include these internal status notes in an outgoing offer as if they resolved the missing evidence.

## 1. One accountable lead, with decisions grouped for PEFC

The proposed lead supplier would own the integrated plan, dependencies, technical quality and coordination of delivery partners. The final team annex must identify the dedicated project manager, design, engineering, migration, testing and service responsibilities, together with actual availability and subcontractor boundaries. No person or allocation is confirmed by this draft. Remote delivery is permitted, but it does not remove the requirement for project coordination during CET/CEST working hours. A proposed weekly written update and decision meeting would combine progress, upcoming approvals, risks and actions; the frequency is a proposal, not an issuer-prescribed cadence. Reviews would be grouped around information architecture, design, CMS demonstration and launch readiness to reduce demands on PEFC's limited internal resources. Each decision would record the responsible reviewer, evidence, date needed and impact of delay. PEFC approves business and editorial decisions; the supplier retains technical testing responsibility. (RFP physical29–30; Q&A67,69.)

The baseline would distinguish initial pefc.org/CMS launch from the ten national migrations, targeting the issuer's 18–24 months following platform launch. The actual schedule must follow verified team allocation and extraction/dependency assessment, not an assumed reuse of another bid's dates. Each site's plan would identify local content review, user acceptance, authorisation to cut over and stabilisation. Required central full-scope responsibility is not replaced by offering migration alone. (RFP physical11,30; Q&A58–59,69.)

## 2. Preserve the Label Generator without taking over its contract

The website project excludes maintenance, redevelopment and technical separation of the Label Generator. Its existing provider continues to maintain that application and supporting infrastructure while it remains on Tatamee. Before any website cutover, the proposed delivery team would map shared authentication/accounts, database, file/CDN references and public pages with the responsible parties. The dependency register would assign a change owner, a compatibility check and a rollback condition to each affected shared service. Website migration would not be treated as authority to shut down shared infrastructure or invalidate historical media references.

A launch decision would require evidence that agreed public links and affected shared dependencies remain usable, plus the existing provider's coordination where relevant. Any additional transition assistance would require a separately agreed scope and price; the normal obligation to coordinate and avoid disruption remains in the core delivery plan. The application's anticipated separate migration during 2027 is not a guaranteed date or a prerequisite we can assume is already satisfied. (RFP physical29; Q&A8–11.)

## 3. Service coverage starts at launch, not the next January

The required initial two-year maintenance/support/development arrangement uses calendar years, with the initial partial-year treatment agreed separately. The service plan must avoid a gap between launch, stabilisation and the first full calendar-year agreement. As each national website joins, it must enter the same managed service inventory with disclosed effects on recurring charges. Warranty defects, per-launch stabilisation and routine maintenance are separate concepts; the final offer must state the duration, arrangements and costs for each rather than implying one substitutes for the others. (RFP physical30–32; Q&A48,60.)

The proposed support workflow uses PEFC International as the normal intake and coordination point for national requests. Each request would record site, impact, start time, severity, assigned responder, next update and disposition. An outage/security-critical incident would trigger technical triage, containment/recovery, stakeholder updates and a closing incident record. Routine editor problems would follow the same visible queue without being confused with optional development. Automated monitoring would cover availability, technical failures, performance, security, unusual traffic and infrastructure expenditure, rather than relying on PEFC staff to notice outages.

Mandatory staffed support is 08:30–17:30 CET/CEST Monday–Friday regardless of supplier location. The final offer must identify actual primary and backup coverage, the holiday arrangement, severity definitions, response/resolution targets and proportionate outside-hours critical support, with associated costs. This draft deliberately supplies no numeric targets or 24/7 promise: neither capacity nor supplier economics is verified. A timezone-aware rota should follow European daylight-saving transitions, not a permanent conversion to Eastern time. Coverage must be evidenced before any “fully meets” classification. (RFP physical31; Q&A48,67.)

## 4. Controlled releases, recoverability and expenditure visibility

The proposed release record would link the change request, affected sites, tests, approval, deployment version and rollback instructions. Shared functionality and security changes would first be exercised in a staging environment accessible to PEFC for review and acceptance; the CMS must separately provide straightforward content preview. One staging environment per national website is not required. Shared releases would include regression checks across national variants and notice of significant editorial or functionality changes. Urgent security changes need an agreed expedited path; emergency handling does not excuse an absent accountable responder or untested access boundaries. (Q&A44–45.)

The final infrastructure decision must state where content, personal data, processing and backups reside, identify subprocessors and explain transfers outside Switzerland/EEA and the relevant safeguards. PEFC-owned hosting/account control is preferred, with the supplier responsible for management, monitoring, updates and recovery. No hosting provider is selected or declared compliant by this draft. Backup frequency, retention, restore targets and test method must be specified and costed by the actual delivery team. A restore rehearsal would be accepted against recorded recovery evidence, not a provider's marketing statement. (Q&A40–43,51–52.)

Cost-control reporting would distinguish human demand, automated traffic, infrastructure usage and supplier charges. Proposed controls include usage alerts, bot-abuse mitigation, a recurring spend review and escalation before material cost growth. Actual thresholds and mitigation mechanisms remain architecture-dependent. The October CDN quantity is not a validated website-only sizing input; unresolved shared-service usage must not become a guaranteed hosting allowance. (Q&A39,41,52.)

## 5. A visible development budget, not unapproved extra work

The annual arrangement must cover maintenance/support plus an agreed ongoing development allowance, whether presented as a combined fee or separate allocations. PEFC sets priorities. The proposed work ledger would show approved scope, estimate, actual effort, remaining allowance and completion evidence. Small tasks within the agreed arrangement could use a proportionate request/approval workflow rather than a formal quote for every item. Larger changes or anything creating additional charges require an estimate and PEFC approval before work starts, including delivery implications and recurring licensing/maintenance effects.

Unused separately allocated development funds do not carry into the next year. National-only enhancements require PEFC coordination and approval; where agreed, the member may be invoiced directly, without silently charging central maintenance twice. The commercial file must identify allowance units, limits, rates, exclusions, renewal terms and possible adjustments. No rate, development quantity, automatic increase or payment milestone is supplied here. (RFP physical31–32; Q&A49–51,60–62.)

## 6. Train the central team and make exit practical

The supplier would initially train PEFC International staff in administration and routine editorial tasks: content, navigation, blocks, media, translations and permissions. Online sessions should be recorded where possible. PEFC remains responsible for national-member day-to-day training and editorial guidance; the supplier provides usable documentation and significant-change updates that PEFC can adapt. The final proposal must identify actual training effort, audience, materials and additional costs without converting this into an unpriced promise to train all national editors directly. (RFP physical32; Q&A46.)

The handover index would cover architecture, CMS configuration, content models, integrations, hosting, deployment, backup/recovery, incident procedures and maintenance responsibilities. PEFC must have source code, relevant configuration and necessary administrative access to change provider. Accounts/licences should be held in PEFC's name where practical. The proposed exit rehearsal would have an authorised replacement operator follow the documentation to locate source/configuration and demonstrate an agreed recovery or deployment task in a non-production environment. It would record restrictions, missing access, dependencies and any separately disclosed transfer cost. The final legal/IP/licence position remains unverified; no exclusivity, unrestricted third-party rights or zero-cost exit is asserted. (Q&A47,51–52.)
