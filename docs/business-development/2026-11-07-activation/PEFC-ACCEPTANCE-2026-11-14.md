# PEFC — migration assurance and acceptance appendix

PRIVATE EDITABLE WORKING DRAFT · BD-022 · 14 November 2026 · PARTIAL / BLOCKED

Conditional proposal text, not an approved offer or a complete response. This appendix extends the 04ET design/migration narrative; it does not replace St. Lucie's owner-reviewed v3 or select PEFC as the primary pursuit. Delivery roles, capacity, architecture, schedule, fees and company qualifications remain unverified. Proposed acceptance controls below are bidder suggestions to agree with PEFC, not issuer-approved thresholds or completed tests.

## 1. Start from evidence, not an assumed complete inventory

The migration approach would begin with the issuer's October 6 URL annex and 2025 historical traffic annex, supplemented by an authorised structured CMS export during project planning. The saved migration-control register assigns stable inventory identifiers to all 9,184 source URL rows while preserving the original domain, URL and page/file classification. It deliberately leaves destination, editorial disposition, language relationships, media dependencies, source record identifier and acceptance evidence unresolved. A row in this register means only that the source lists a URL: no content has been downloaded, imported, approved or successfully migrated.

The separate 88-row historical-journey register compares each historical path with the sitemap for the same site, normalising the site's leading www and a trailing slash. Scheme, query and fragment are outside this path-only comparison; original URLs remain preserved. This is a case-sensitive path comparison, not a complete URL-equivalence or live HTTP test. Of those rows, 56 have a sitemap path match and 32 do not. The unmatched set includes all ten Belgian entries because Belgium is absent from the supplied sitemap, plus roots and paths on other sites. Historical paths may have changed, been redirected, been omitted by sitemap rules or represented excluded content types. They must not be labelled broken or deleted without evidence. A matched path likewise proves neither preserved content nor working functionality.

The supplier would reconcile the published baseline with actual exports and PEFC's editorial decisions before agreeing migration effort. People, member organisations, landing pages and Belgium need explicit inventory treatment; the public sitemap does not cover them comprehensively. Published source content and its associated media are the estimating basis under Q56, not every unpublished draft or unused binary. A file URL is not a unique stored file, and neither its title nor a successful download establishes document accessibility. Unknown quantities remain explicit assumptions rather than zero-cost exclusions.

## 2. Retain editorial accountability while reducing manual work

A structured migration mapping would define each source content type, required fields, relationships, embedded blocks, language links and destination template. Automated or semi-automated extraction and transformation would be used where supported by the actual export, with representative trial imports reviewed before any production cutover. Extraction access, privacy safeguards and tooling would require agreement with the existing provider; no public URL list gives authority to access the current CMS or its nonpublic records.

The proposed division of responsibility is: the supplier prepares technical extraction, mapping, trial imports, URL proposals, validation reports and defect corrections; PEFC International and national members make retain/update/consolidate/remove decisions and approve content. The existing provider supplies or facilitates agreed source access and continuity within its existing remit. No named person or subcontractor is allocated by this draft. Review packs would group decisions by site and content family, with duplicate decisions reused only when PEFC approves the relationship. Technical exceptions would be reported as evidence-linked items rather than requiring PEFC staff to re-enter every page manually.

The ledger would preserve source-to-destination traceability even when several pages consolidate into one. Redirects would follow an approved mapping rather than a blanket home-page redirect. After implementation, checks would record requested URL, observed status, redirect hops, destination, language and outcome; approved removals would have their own disposition. Exact query-string and fragment behaviour would be considered where it affects a real journey, rather than assuming the path-only join resolves it.

## 3. Test visitor journeys and editorial tasks separately

The historical-journey annex provides a prioritisation signal, not a substitute for complete functional acceptance. It would seed tests for certification navigation, standards and document discovery, news, national-language navigation and other source-listed paths. Belgium's French and Dutch journeys would be covered even though its sitemap is absent. Finland and Poland would receive defined acceptance checks despite having no rows in the historical traffic annex. Historical visits are not a forecast, unique-person count, conversion measure or proof of buyer interest.

The fourteen supplied mockup pages would inform reusable components and the implementation of the existing design direction. Responsive states, keyboard operation, screen-reader behaviour, loading/errors, empty search results, pagination and accessible alternatives to map interactions would be specified before acceptance. Actual approved authentic imagery would be used; the brand guide's prohibition on AI-generated imagery remains binding independently of the RFP's welcome for responsible AI-assisted development. Photography permissions, official logo files and font assets still require validation. No visual concept or generated image is created in this shift.

Editor acceptance would include creating a page without code, media selection, previewing a draft, publishing, revising and managing straightforward redirects. Under Q24, formal multistage approval within the CMS is not mandatory: PEFC generally reviews and approves content outside the CMS before uploading. In-CMS submission/approval would be tested only if separately proposed and agreed; it is not a prerequisite for the mandatory editor workflow. Cross-national permission checks would verify that a national editor cannot change another site's content, settings or private media, while centrally shared assets can be used without modifying or deleting their originals. Server enforcement, direct URL access and cache behaviour would be tested, not merely whether controls are hidden in the interface. No CMS is selected and none of these tasks has been executed.

The optional Find my standard quiz would remain outside core inclusion unless separately selected, scoped and priced. Likewise native advanced forms, advanced newsletter transfer, Dynamics integration, SSO, Canva integration, independent penetration testing and independent accessibility certification would not silently enter core commitments. Optional status does not remove mandatory ordinary forms, subscription integration, security testing, accessibility or authentication requirements. Core integration acceptance would explicitly exercise the Find Certified iframe, clear Label Generator links, per-site contact routing, site-specific Matomo/consent settings, externally hosted video and newsletter subscriptions. The plan would also cover event timezone conversion, external registration links and ICS export. These are source requirements, not evidence of implemented integrations; exact authorised endpoints and credentials remain unresolved. The acceptance register distinguishes these boundaries so that decorative mockups do not become unpriced functionality.

## 4. Prove transition safety before each cutover

The initial pefc.org launch and subsequent national migrations would have separate approval gates. National onboarding would reuse shared platform capabilities but retain site-specific content, permissions, domain coordination, language and integration checks. The RFP objective is to complete national migrations within 18–24 months following the new platform launch; this draft gives no unsupported staffing-dependent launch date or guaranteed migration throughput.

Before each launch, the proposed evidence pack would contain the approved content/redirect disposition register, automated and manual validation results, unresolved risks and their approved treatment, editor acceptance records, a deployment/cutover record and recovery instructions. Accessibility coverage would include shared templates/components and representative migrated journeys, with editor-owned content obligations described separately. Automated findings alone would not be presented as WCAG 2.2 AA conformance. Final tolerances, sampling, severity definitions and launch authority remain to be agreed.

The transition must preserve Tatamee for unmigrated sites and protect Label Generator dependencies. Label Generator maintenance, redevelopment and technical separation remain outside this RFP; any additional supplier transition work would require separate agreement and pricing. A dependency record would identify shared authentication, databases, storage/CDN and public routes before changes. DNS changes would be coordinated with the domain administrators; national members retain domain responsibility. Rollback would consider changes made after cutover and source/destination reconciliation, rather than assume a DNS reversal restores all newly edited content.

## 5. Operational acceptance and handover

A proposed operational rehearsal would cover matched database/media/configuration restoration, protected backups, appropriate single-site recovery, deployment rollback, notification/escalation, permissions and unpublished-content isolation. The responsible team would supply measured recovery evidence and state backup frequency, retention and expected recovery times. No measured recovery time, uptime, publication delay or critical response target is asserted here. Those values require architecture, supplier, staffing and commercial evidence.

Ongoing coverage must include weekday 08:30–17:30 CET/CEST support, monitoring, security maintenance, cost/bot-traffic controls, an agreed annual development allowance and explicit out-of-hours arrangements. Actual coverage and costs remain gates, not an inference from a remote supplier or an AI tool. Stabilisation and warranty arrangements would be stated separately from the initial two-year maintenance/support/development agreement, including the initial partial-calendar-year treatment and renewal terms.

Training would initially equip PEFC International to administer the platform and perform editorial tasks; PEFC retains day-to-day national-editor training and guidance under Q46. Technical handover would include source/configuration access, account control, deployment instructions, integrations, recovery and current documentation, sufficient to transfer technical management. A separate CHF/EUR commercial file would cover core build, all ten national migrations and recurring costs in the three-year comparison, with optional services and taxes explicit. This appendix contains no commercial quotation or cross-bid price transfer.
